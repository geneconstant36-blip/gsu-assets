The Media Detective Card

Your attention is valuable, and companies spend billions of dollars trying to trap it. Three questions turn you from someone being sold to into someone doing the looking. Stick this next to the screen.

The Popcorn Brain trap. Ever picked up a tablet to look up one quick fact for homework, and suddenly forty-five minutes are gone and you're watching cats fall off furniture? It happens to everyone. When you scroll fast through short videos, your brain gets used to tiny bursts of excitement — and then it's hard to sit down and read a book, solve a tough puzzle, or build something complicated. That's the trap. Here's how you get out of it.

Ask these three before you click

QUESTION 1
Who made this?
A friend? A teacher? Or a company trying to sell you something?
QUESTION 2
How does it want me to feel?
Is it trying to make you angry, scared, or jealous so you keep watching?
QUESTION 3
What is the hook?
Look at the title, or the first three seconds. What trick is stopping you scrolling away?
The 10-Minute Challenge — the Hard Stop

Next time you catch yourself scrolling without thinking: turn the screen off, walk outside, and look at something far away. Ten minutes. That's it. Your eyes rest, your thoughts slow down, and you get the steering wheel back.

Caught one? Write it down. Three hooks this week is a good week for a detective.
