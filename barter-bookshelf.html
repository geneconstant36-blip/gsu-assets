Teach, Then Play

The Instrument Bench — the four moves of every trade

Every barter, from a loaf-for-a-lesson to a three-party loop, runs on the same four moves. Each instrument teaches one move in thirty seconds, then drills you on it. When all four feel natural, the Climb is waiting.

1 · The Want Matcher

The double coincidence of wants

A direct trade only closes when both sides match: they have what you want and want what you have. Half a match is no match — that friction is the very reason money was invented.

2 · The Trade Equalizer

Cost · price · value

Retail price is a clue, not a verdict. A fair trade balances utility and scarcity — what the thing does, for whom, right now. A spare generator is clutter in June and continuity the week before a storm.

3 · The Loop Forger

The tri-trade

When no pair of wants matches, a circle can: the plumber fixes the baker's sink, the baker feeds the welder, the welder repairs the plumber's trailer. Three trades, zero dollars, nobody needed a matching partner.

4 · The Word Bench

Scope, deliverable, settlement

Most barter disputes are scope disputes. The master trader names the deliverable, the standard, and the settlement before effort is spent — fairness is built through transparent terms, not found in a number.
